02 · Cross-Border Systems

Regulated information,
with its evidence attached.

Consular rules, market closures, statutory deadlines, official statistics. Information that is scattered across jurisdictions by default, and where a wrong answer has a cost measured in money or in someone missing a flight.

These are not information-design problems. They are governance problems that arrive wearing an interface. The common commitment across all three: every fact carries its source, and an estimate is returned as an estimate rather than as a confident wrong answer.

Built work

PropertyWhat it isStatus

dipl.pro Cross-border relocation intelligence. Consular, visa and emergency information consolidated from dozens of unrelated government sites into one guided answer, organised by urgency rather than by visual hierarchy.In build
orbisdata.org A directory and field guide to the world's official statistics — who publishes what, on what basis, and how far it can be trusted.In build

Where a rule is announced rather than computed, we say so rather than guess.

The design position

Most calendars and directories hand you a date or a figure. These hand you the date, what stands behind it, and how sure anyone can be. That is the provenance commitment made operational — and it is the only defensible way to build a system that someone else's decision will rest on.
