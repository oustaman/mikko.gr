03 · Governance

Ambiguity is a cost.
Someone always pays it.

Where a judgement is repeated by many people across many cases, the quality of the outcome depends less on the people than on whether the rule was ever written down.

Flat structures and unstated criteria are very often the visible residue of an organisation that could not resolve an internal disagreement and shipped the disagreement instead. Making the rule explicit is usually a governance act wearing a design costume — which is why these projects stall on a decision, not on a deliverable.

Built work

Classification toolingDecision rules for assessment — removing interpretation from repeated judgements, producing outcomes that are faster and defensible on appeal.
Workflow automationBulk document generation across weighted components, automating double-marking reconciliation. Roughly 300 staff hours recovered per term.
Reconciliation systemsCollaboration tooling for multi-marker workflows, reducing a multi-step manual process to a single reviewable page.

The pattern

Each of these started as an interface request and turned out to be a question about who decides what, on what basis, and who can contest it. The interface is where that becomes visible — and where it can be fixed without a restructure.
