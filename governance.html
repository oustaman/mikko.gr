Governance

Ambiguity is a cost.
Someone always pays it.

Where a judgement is repeated across many cases by many people, the quality of the outcome depends less on the people than on whether the rule was ever written down.

A flat structure is often the visible residue of an organisation that could not resolve a disagreement and shipped it instead. Making the rule explicit is a governance act wearing a design costume — which is why this work stalls on a decision, not on a deliverable.

  • Classification systems — decision rules that remove interpretation from repeated judgements
  • Workflow automation — reconciliation across weighted components; roughly 300 hours recovered per term
  • Taxonomy governance — versioned, sourced, contestable
