01 · Market Entry

Products cross borders.
Interfaces don't.

Information density is not a cultural preference. It is a functional response to conditions — and it does not survive the journey between markets equally in both directions.

Compression
Maximum meaning in minimum space. A contract: the interface gives less, the user supplies the remainder. Efficient, and dependent on a reader who can complete it.
Clarity
Meaning spread across several encodings. Costs space and time; survives a reader who arrives without the shared context.
The asymmetry
A clarified product entering a dense market is verbose. A compressed product entering a clarified market is suspicious.

Verbose is a criticism. Suspicious is a verdict.

It is a trust failure, not a comprehension failure

The distinction is expensive. A company that believes its users are confused responds by adding explanation — which adds density, and makes the real problem worse while appearing to address the stated one.

Densityreads as low status — discount shops shout, premium brands whisper
Simultaneityreads as an organisation that has not decided what it does
No whitespacereads as no editorial judgement — nothing was rejected
Dense small printreads as concealment — and lands at checkout, where it costs most

The regulatory floor

Since June 2025 the European Accessibility Act has applied to products and services placed on the EU market — e-commerce, banking, e-books, ticketing, consumer hardware — by where they are sold, not where the company sits. Its technical requirements are, almost item for item, decompression requirements: redundant encoding, sequential structure, explicit hierarchy, adequate spacing.

Which means the mandated work and the revenue-generating work are the same work. That is unusual, and it is the argument I make to boards who have filed accessibility under legal rather than commercial.
