Decision Architecture

I design the decisions,
then I build the thing.

Every interface is a place where someone decides something. A menu is a hypothesis about what they will choose next. An information structure is, quietly, a position on what gets seen. Designing those environments on purpose is the work.

Twenty years across graphic design, product and cross-border systems — seven of them running a consultancy in Hong Kong, and a doctorate in design. What the behavioural tradition theorises, a designer builds: the actual screen, structure and sequence through which a real person moves toward a choice.

Commitments

Argument
A structure makes a case about who the user is and what they came to do. Designing it well means making that case legible — and defensible.
Grain, not rank
Ranking imposes one decision on everyone. Organising by grain returns the decision to the reader. The refusal to rank is itself a design ethic.
Provenance
A decision is only as good as what it rests on. Every fact carries its source.
State-first
The page is the state. Build from what is true now, dependency-free, so nothing stands between the reader and the choice.
